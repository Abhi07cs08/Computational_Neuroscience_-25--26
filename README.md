# Comp Neuro Research Project

### Overview
Representational alignment between biological and
artificial neural networks is typically evaluated by how well
artificial model representations predict biological neural activity.
Recent work has shown that this relationship is asymmetric:
model representations predict neural responses much better than
neural responses predict model representations. This asymmetry
suggests that representational geometry may influence how learned
representations are organized. We hypothesized that steering
representational geometry during learning can systematically
influence bidirectional representational alignment. To test this
hypothesis, we developed a computational framework that
integrates spectral regularization with bidirectional predictivity
analyses. As an initial demonstration, we evaluated our framework
using self-supervised contrastive vision models. Steering represen-
tational geometry substantially increased reverse predictivity while
decreasing forward predictivity, resulting in a net improvement in
bidirectional predictivity. These improvements were accompanied
by a reorganization of the representational subspace shared
by biological and artificial neural networks and a reduction
in effective dimensionality, suggesting a potential mechanism
underlying the improved alignment. Overall, these findings
demonstrate the utility of our framework for systematically
studying the role of representational geometry in bidirectional
representational alignment, contributing to a deeper mechanistic
understanding of intelligence in biological and artificial neural
networks.
